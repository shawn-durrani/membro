Mathematics

The machinery of your memory, drawn live from your real data. Two words to know: each remembered fact is one card; the ledger is the append-only book holding every card ever written — superseded and quarantined ones included, nothing erased. Nothing on this page shows what any card says — every dot and filament is pure geometry: an id, an age, an importance, a score. This is what the math is doing underneath, made visible.

The life of your memory

orbit — biomes & memories

Your memory as it is right now. Each dot is one memory, sized by importance and coloured by its weight (the same ramp as the ledger); the translucent coloured volumes are its biomes — the neighbourhood of related memories around it. Drag to orbit; zoom in to reveal the trails between memories that get recalled together, and click a dot or a biome to see what it buried (its sediment).

What you're seeing: one real recall — your question fans across every card in the ledger (the tall column) and the strongest few flow into the ranked answers, tick brightness showing how much each resonated. Why it matters: the model weighs your whole history every time, not one lookup (white won, amber lost as a near-duplicate); press ▶ evolve to watch the answer change through history. On screen: the rest of this page draws shapes, never the words in your memories, but "follow live recalls" replays each question as it was typed, so untick it before you share your screen.

The forgetting curve

today

What you're seeing: the forgetting curve — each memory's height is how strongly it still competes to stay in your profile, and it sinks as it ages. Why it matters: important memories fade about 4× slower than trivia, so the surface shows what's quietly slipping away; drag the slider to fast-forward.
